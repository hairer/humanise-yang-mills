## 1. What the committee says is proved and not proved, and how it maps to the monograph's target

### 1.1 The committee's headline (p. 1)

(Q) a.txt 11–15: "This validation covers the first OpenAI Lean submission which formalizes the continuum pure Yang–Mills theory. In our judgment the submission proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms can be constructed as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory for any simple compact gauge group. It does not prove that the infinite-volume limit is unique or that there is a mass gap."

(Q) a.txt 16–25: "The proof constructs continuum correlation functions as a scaling limit of four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory correlation functions at a fixed physical volume. Subsequential convergence to an infinite-volume limit is then proven using compactness, and this limit is not proven to be unique. Any chosen subsequential limit admits a decomposition into component theories (pure phases), each proven to satisfy the Osterwalder–Schrader axioms. Then, through the OS reconstruction theorem (also proven in the submission) this defines a QFT satisfying the Wightman axioms. Its lattice origin, local fields, normalized stress tensor, leading short-distance laws and operator product expansion provide substantial evidence that the construction describes Yang–Mills theory. We believe that the failure to prove uniqueness of the large volume limit and of the vacuum is not inconsistent with the general expectation that the infinite-volume limit and the true Yang–Mills vacuum are unique."

(Q) a.txt 28–32: "OpenAI's construction does not certify the full Jaffe–Witten problem: no mass gap is claimed. Uniqueness across volume subsequences and agreement of the component theories are also unresolved. Under the existence criterion adopted here, we are not requiring a precise characterization of the vacuum state."

(Q) a.txt 39–45, on the basis of the assessment: "This assessment uses the Lean sources submitted by OpenAI on Oct 28, 2026 … It does not draw on the separate informal proof manuscript. We used OpenAI's Astra to assist with the digestion of the supplied Lean code. We do not claim to fully understand the proof yet. … The OpenAI work rests on prior work by many physicists and mathematicians. A full review with careful attribution will be provided in later releases by the Yang–Mills group."

Relevance. (I) The committee's words "proves that a relativistic quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms can be constructed" are a judgment about the Lean development. The monograph says of itself (M, `assembly.tex` last paragraph) "this article does not contain a complete existence proof". There is no contradiction (one is about the formal proof, the other about the prose), but any monograph sentence citing the assessment should keep the two apart. The sentence "We do not claim to fully understand the proof yet" is worth quoting wherever the monograph relies on the assessment for mathematical explanation.

### 1.2 The requirements (Section 1, p. 3) and what the construction provides (Section 2, p. 3)

(Q) a.txt 107–119: "Four features of a constructed Yang–Mills theory are required in the problem statement by Jaffe–Witten. [JW] 1. A nontrivial theory satisfying QFT axioms. The space of operators must correspond to gauge-invariant polynomials in the curvature F and its covariant derivatives. 3. The predicted short-distance behavior. Renormalized local operators corresponding to gauge-invariant polynomials in F and its covariant derivatives are defined, including a conserved stress-tensor. The construction defines the trace of the stress tensor `YangMills.Hierarchy.centeredTrace` and proves a number of properties about it in `YangMills.Hierarchy.Properties.nonfree`. 4. ### 2.5 Does anything in it visibly exclude a generalized-free-field witness?

Two different questions must be separated.

(a) Is the G01 mechanism (constant insertions, an artificial second phase, a hierarchy supplied by a generalized free scalar field) available against the combined target? (I) No, as far as the text shows, and for a structural reason: the target does not quantify over arbitrary insertions or an arbitrary hierarchy. The insertions are the family's own cylinder functions of its local sections (definitional, `actual_insertion_formula` is `rfl`), `Makes` constrains the sections and the RG path, the table S must be the ordered limit of the family's Wilson moments, the phase expectations must be characters of the ground-state algebra of S's own OS space, and the reconstructed theory must be the boundary value of the selected phase. G01's own records say the same of `constructed` (README finding 1: "the detailed theorem `YangMills.constructed` does not admit this construction"). Caveat: I did not open the definitions of `Sound`, `Selected`, `Based`, `Ordered`, or `Certificate`; whether a degenerate `Family` (for instance one whose local sections are trivial) could satisfy `Makes` is (U). The clauses `local_content` (field spaces isomorphic to the Maxwell sector), `faithful` and the positive trace law would have to be met by it as well. (Q) G01 README Revision 2: "None of the G01 results applies to the stronger target; whether that target admits a witness without Yang–Mills content was not examined." That remains the state after this report.

(b) Does the combined target exclude that the constructed continuum theory *is* (a mixture of) generalized free fields? (Q) No clause is claimed to do so. Assessment a.txt 561–562 (quoted above); `LeadingTrace-20261028.lean`, "Limits of this example": "The positive leading coefficient excludes an identically zero trace correlator. It does not by itself exclude every generalized free field or every composite of Gaussian fields, establish all-orders perturbative matching, determine an intrinsic reference mass, or settle the vacuum structure. The lattice, field, Ward and reconstruction checks must be read together with this observable test." The source's `TraceExclusions` (retained in `SourceConclusions`) has, besides the mass-shell profile exclusion, clauses named `wick`, `maxwell` and `invariant` (M, `HeadlineSpec.lean`), which exclude further specified classes; the assessment does not describe them and I did not analyse them. In this sense the question "would simply not arise" is half right: the specification-level question disappears because the lattice family is the construction's own, but the mathematical question whether the limit is generalized free stays open and the committee says so.

# Independent review of variable-stage comparison windows

Reviewed `17-variable-windows.tex` against the installed Chapter11 response, covariance and extraction arguments and the prescribed-radius demands in Chapter17. This is a review of this extension, not a fresh certification of the entire marked expansion.

The pre-ratio split has identifiable suppliers: the difference-through-response row estimate explicitly fixes R before L (Chapter11 near `finite-covariance`); the leading polynomial recurrence fixes its coefficient amplification before selecting L (the paragraph choosing kappa,q,D); the marked interpolation/column estimate has its ratio floor selected subsequently. Fixed-ratio stationary jets, incidence and path constants remain entry-delay costs. The product estimate log product(C+2) <= sup epsilon_stage times total logarithmic window length consequently gives H^{o(1)} even when the number of crossed stages is unbounded: every level in the window, hence its minimum stage, tends to infinity.

The adjacent covariance identity `ch11:conditional-curvature` retains both actual neighboring metrics and is explicitly valid across a stage boundary. Iteration therefore telescopes with products of unequal actual responses; no common fictitious metric is required. The separate actual-history finite-covariance estimate and protected-path error bounds are the ones used at the endpoints. The top extraction T is defined by the original insertion and its same-insertion covariance and is independent of middle/output stops, as stated in the finite-arity proof; restricting one longest restarted history is consistent with this definition.

The window length O(log H) is smaller than the restarted-history short interval; overshoots from L_i <= log(beta_hat_i) are o(log H). The positive comparison-error powers and strict strip/mesh inequalities leave room for H^{o(1)} losses. The all-copy root exponent can be weakened from 1/(16 n) to 1/(32 n). The phase conclusion uses the previously proved compression-algebra moment norm, rather than a new phase-dependent estimate.

Requested two editorial clarifications from the author: write the stage ratio as mathscr L_j, with J_i=J(i+1), to distinguish it from the level ratio L_i; explicitly state that post-ratio constants enter additive local errors or a bounded number of endpoint factors, not a product once per step. Entry bounds by H^{epsilon_j} alone would not justify such a product over O(log H) steps. The current recurrence references support the intended additive use; no additional mathematical failure was identified in this check.

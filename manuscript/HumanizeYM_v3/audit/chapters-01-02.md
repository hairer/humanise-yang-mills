The factor a_N^{4n} in smearing is physical volume measure. Time last; axes 1…4. R_p is physical period and L_m is ratio; scales become finer with increasing m.

The explicit Casimir identity is standard finite-dimensional Lie theory; no new proof included. The classical plaquette expansion is stated using exact ODE holonomy, not endpoint exponentials. Finite-volume RP is restricted to β≥0, even torus, gauge-invariant bounded Borel functions, and links wholly in the specified positive sector, matching Martin `prop:ex-lattice-finite-reflection`. Both site/link reflection pullbacks include inversion. A restricted seam-free chessboard setting uses s=2qℓ, open cell interiors and nonnegative reflected copies |F|. No extension to arbitrary seam observables or arbitrary tilings is claimed.

## Chapter 2

**Local-section theorem.** Sources: Scott `part2/A-section.tex`, `lem:A1` / `eq:A-average`; Martin `existence/local-sections.tex`, finite sampler, equivariant recovery and `eq:local-section-average`. Inputs: fixed compact matrix G, finite d, radial jet space and invariant real bases. Output: finite period-independent stencil, linear polynomial section, correct weighted Taylor map, unit/reality/H₄ covariance, monomials of column i with at least f_i curvature factors. The argument was checked directly across both prose sources. No transfer theorem or continuum field-null statement enters.

**Proof details checked.** The derivative of a transported curvature at zero is its plaquette circulation since the zero curvature kills transport derivatives. Vanishing at every integer base point implies vanishing polynomial circulation; its top homogeneous part is dA_k, and radial contraction gives (k+2)A_k. Finite-dimensional separation gives finitely many measurements. Equivariant projection followed by inverse function theorem gives an inverse germ. Its Taylor polynomial has zero constant term, so substitution preserves the minimum factor count. Ordinary Taylor remainder has no low engineering-weight contribution since jet coordinates have positive weight ≥2. H₄ averaging uses inverse action on coefficient labels and pullback on links. Curvature transforms by a sign/conjugation under signed lattice isometries. Repeating covering links on a small torus preserves the polynomial identity.

**Uniform bounds.** Sources: Scott `lem:A-bounds`; Martin `eq:local-section-column-price`. Threshold bound uses only the finitely many plaquettes actually read by a section; later support conventions may impose the stronger requirement that all plaquettes meeting the declared support are small. Directions are bounded by t(1+periodic distance)^p₀. A derivative of order k of a fixed path expression is O(t^k), uniformly in torus period and placement. Each curvature factor or its derivative is O(t) when 0<t≤1; product rule gives t^{f_i}. Constants may depend on G, chosen sections, d,q,p₀, but not period, placement or threshold. Global polynomial bounds supply the separate large-field estimate. No summability over degrees, arities or cutoffs is claimed.

**Literature.** Exact local-section construction searched before proof; no older exact theorem was located in the searches recorded in `foundations-literature.md`. This is not a novelty claim. Ordinary analytic ODE parameter dependence and inverse-function steps are imported (Teschl2012 and Rudin1976); Rudin Theorem 9.24 is the inverse-function citation supplied by Scott, with analytic upgrade standard and not separately attributed as a new result. The full general analytic-theorem pages have not been independently inspected in this pass. All cited keys already occur in Scott's bibliography.

**Further checks before final acceptance.** Add an original radial/Fock–Schwinger-gauge reference if that formula is to carry historical attribution; no proof of the standard radial identity is included. Validate local sections against any exact downstream norm convention once the RG chapters fix it; the draft states its own complete specialized bound and does not assert an unspecified 'admissibility'. Compare derivative left/right ordering against downstream definitions. The source uses the same left convention.

**Examples.** Clover: 24 corner plaquettes, coefficient 1/4, each leading term h⁴|F_μν|²/2; gives c_{F²}=¼Σ_μν |F_μν|². Additive mixing is described but no terminal map assumed. The free equation ideal is explicitly not identified with the interacting null ideal. Grade-tag distinctions are retained.
